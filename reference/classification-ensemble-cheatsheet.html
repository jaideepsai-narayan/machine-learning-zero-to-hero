The Grand Evolution of Machine Learning Algorithms

Topic: Algorithm Evolution, Drawbacks & Transitions ← Lesson 0008

Every major algorithm in machine learning was invented to fix a specific flaw in the algorithm before it. Below is the complete evolutionary timeline from simple lines to gradient boosted trees:

1. Linear Regression

Goal & Formula: Fit a continuous straight line $y = m \cdot x + b$ by minimizing Mean Squared Error (MSE).

⚠️ Major Drawback: Outputs unbounded continuous numbers ($-\infty, +\infty$). It cannot handle binary (Yes/No) classification or predict probabilities.
💡 Why Move to Next Algorithm? We need a function to squish continuous outputs into probabilities between 0.0 and 1.0 $\implies$ Logistic Regression.

2. Logistic Regression

Goal & Formula: Pass linear output through the Sigmoid S-curve: $\hat{p} = \frac{1}{1 + e^{-(mx+b)}}$, minimizing Log Loss.

⚠️ Major Drawback: Can only draw a single linear (flat) decision boundary in feature space. Completely fails on non-linearly separable data (e.g., XOR patterns or complex nested features) without manual feature engineering.
💡 Why Move to Next Algorithm? We need non-linear decision boundaries that automatically partition feature space without manual math transformations $\implies$ Decision Trees.

3. Decision Trees

Goal & Formula: Recursively split data using feature threshold questions that maximize Information Gain (or minimize Gini Impurity $G = 1 - \sum p_i^2$).

⚠️ Major Drawback: High Variance & Overfitting! A deep decision tree memorizes noise in the training data, resulting in poor generalization on unseen test data.
💡 Why Move to Next Algorithm? We need to reduce high variance by averaging hundreds of independent trees trained on random subsets $\implies$ Bagging & Random Forests.

4. Bagging & Random Forests

Goal & Formula: Train $B$ independent decision trees in parallel using bootstrapped row samples and random feature subsets ($\sqrt{M}$). Aggregate predictions via majority vote or averaging.

⚠️ Major Drawback: 1. Trees are built independently, so hard-to-predict samples get ignored if the majority vote gets them wrong. 2. High Bias if the base trees are too shallow.
💡 Why Move to Next Algorithm? Instead of building trees independently in parallel, build trees sequentially, where each new tree specifically fixes the residual mistakes of all prior trees $\implies$ Boosting (AdaBoost / XGBoost).

5. Boosting (AdaBoost, Gradient Boosting & XGBoost)

Goal & Formula: Build sequential ensembles fitting residuals: $F_m(x) = F_{m-1}(x) + \eta \cdot h_m(x)$.

🏆 Why It Dominates: Combines weak learners into an optimal strong learner, achieving state-of-the-art performance on tabular data!
